\section{Introduction}
This document introduces the charging behaviour of a resistor-capacitor(RC) network. When a capacitor $C$ us charged through a series resistor R from a constant supply $V_0$, the capacitor voltage rises according to

\begin{equation}
    V(t) = V_0(1-e^-t/RC),
    \label{equ:1}
\end{equation}

where the product $RC$ has units of seconds and is called the time constant, $\tau = RC$.

\section{Voltage at the Time Constant}
Substituting $t = \tau$ into \ref{equ:1}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    V(\tau) = V_0(1-e^{-\tau/RC})\\
    =V_0(1-e^{-RC/RC})since\tau=RC\\
    =V_0(1-e^{-1})\\
    \approx V_0(1-0.368)\approx0.632V_0.
\end{aligned}
\label{equ:2}
\end{equation}
The exponent cancelling to -1 in \ref{equ:2} is why the 0.632 fraction is universal -- R and C dropped out of the calculation before the final line, so this fraction holds for any RC network. Substituting $V_0$ = 5.0v:
\begin{equation}
    V(\tau) \approx0.632x5.0V\approx3.16V,
    \label{equ:3}
\end{equation}
matching the dashed line in Figure 1.
